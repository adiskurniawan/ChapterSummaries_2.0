29. Q: What is attention residue, and how does it affect performance?
A: Show answer
Attention residue occurs when thoughts about a previous activity continue occupying attention after an individual has switched to another task. For example, an employee interrupted while preparing a financial analysis may continue thinking about the unresolved issue while attempting to prepare a management report. This reduces the attention available for the new activity and may impair performance. Sophie Leroy's 2009 research examined this phenomenon through experiments involving task switching and found that unresolved work can interfere with subsequent performance. The research highlights why frequent transitions between demanding activities can be counterproductive.
