A Creative Simmer project is an idea or collection of source material that is stored for possible future use without an immediate commitment to develop or finish it. The author compares it to a pot of stock simmering on a low heat: it can remain in the background and may become useful when the right opportunity appears. Unlike a front-burner project, it does not demand active attention. Unlike a back-burner project, it may not have a defined delivery schedule or even a planned completion. Its value lies in preserving material and allowing connections to develop over time without creating an obligation to act.
Study instruction:
- "Identify an idea worth preserving but not yet pursuing and place it in a clearly named simmer or R&D collection."
Key point:
- Simmer projects preserve optionality without requiring immediate execution.
- A simmer project may become useful later or may never become a finished product.
39. Q: How does a simmer project differ from a front-burner or back-burner project?
A: Show answer
A front-burner project requires active creative energy and frequent decisions. A back-burner project has a reliable process and continues through scheduled tasks with limited intervention. A simmer project is stored for possible future development and may have no deadline, defined output, or commitment to finish. The author emphasizes that simmer projects do not need to be completed at all. They preserve ideas and material until an opportunity or a new connection makes them relevant. This category helps creators avoid treating every interesting thought as a project that must be delivered, while still retaining valuable material for future work.
Study instruction:
- "Classify five ideas as front-burner, back-burner, or simmer and justify each classification."
Key point:
- Front-burner work needs active attention; back-burner work follows a routine; simmer work is stored for possibility.
- Do not assign deadlines to ideas that are not yet commitments.

42. Q: How can a creator decide when to move a simmer project into active development?
A: Show answer
A simmer project should move into active work when a clear purpose, opportunity, audience, or question makes further development worthwhile. The creator should review the stored material, assess whether it is still relevant, identify missing information, and estimate the resources required. It may be promoted to a back-burner project if its process becomes repeatable, or to a front-burner project if it requires substantial new creative decisions. If the material no longer serves a useful purpose, it can remain archived or be discarded. The key is to make promotion a deliberate decision rather than allowing every captured idea to become an active commitment automatically.
Study instruction:
- "Write criteria for promoting an R&D idea, including purpose, relevance, available evidence, resources, and next action."
Key point:
- A simmer project becomes active when a real opportunity or purpose justifies the effort.
- Promotion should be deliberate and supported by a clear next step.
