1. Q: Why should a Finance Manager treat day-to-day finance operations as one controlled process rather than separate bookkeeping activities?
A: Show answer
In a not-for-profit environment, accounts payable, accounts receivable, purchasing, corporate credit cards, bank reconciliations, grants, and reporting are connected. The Finance Manager should establish clear ownership, cut-off dates, approval paths, coding rules, and reconciliation procedures across the cycle. For GWAHS, the Team Leader–Finance role specifically brings these operational areas together while also requiring timely and compliant reporting. For restricted research grants, the same principle applies because project expenditure, donor claims, receivables, partner balances, and reporting deadlines depend on reliable underlying transactions. A practical control framework should define who initiates, reviews, approves, posts, reconciles, and closes each transaction type. Power Query can consolidate AP, AR, bank, purchasing, and grant extracts into one management view and identify missing or unmatched records. VBA can generate daily exception lists and month-end task trackers. The Finance Manager should still retain human review over unusual transactions, because automation should improve consistency rather than replace financial judgment.

Send to the team:
- "Please treat AP, AR, purchasing, cards, bank and grant transactions as one controlled finance cycle with clear ownership and reconciliation points."
Key point:
- Integrated finance processes reduce gaps between transaction processing and reporting.
- Automation should surface exceptions while management retains control over approvals and judgment.

2. Q: How would you structure the accounts payable and purchasing control environment in a funded not-for-profit organization?
A: Show answer
The Finance Manager should ensure that a supplier invoice can be traced from purchase request or procurement evidence through approval, receipt of goods or services, accounting entry, payment authorization, and bank settlement. The control should confirm that the expenditure is allowable, correctly coded, within delegated authority, and consistent with the relevant grant or program budget where funding restrictions apply. GWAHS specifically places accounts payable and purchasing within the Finance team's responsibilities and also requires compliance with procurement requirements and financial delegations. For restricted grants, expenditure should additionally be checked against donor conditions and project timelines. A practical control register can include supplier, purchase reference, project, account, budget line, approver, invoice date, due date, payment batch, and supporting-document status. Power Query can combine procurement and AP files to identify invoices without purchase references or approvals. VBA can prepare an exception report for missing evidence and overdue approvals. The Finance Manager should review exception trends rather than merely processing the volume of invoices.

Send to the team:

Key point:
- Accounts payable is both an accounting process and a procurement control.
- Grant-funded purchases require an additional eligibility and budget check.

3. Q: Why should accounts receivable be monitored as actively as accounts payable?
A: Show answer
Receivables represent funding or other amounts expected to be collected, so an accurate ledger is necessary for both liquidity management and grant accountability. A Finance Manager should distinguish between amounts invoiced, formally approved, submitted to a donor, due under an agreement, disputed, and actually collected. In the CIFOR-ICRAF grants role, donor receivables are specifically monitored and collection is followed up, while monthly summaries identify significant donor receivables and payables. The underlying schedule should show grant, donor, claim or invoice reference, amount, due date, collection status, responsible project lead, and any unresolved query. Management should review aging and investigate old items rather than carrying them indefinitely. Power Query can compare receivable schedules with bank receipts and donor-reporting records. VBA can produce an aging dashboard and create follow-up lists. The Finance Manager should also distinguish slow collection caused by administrative delays from amounts that are genuinely disputed or unsupported, because the response differs in each case.

Send to the team:
- "Please maintain an aging schedule for receivables and record the reason, owner, and next action for every overdue balance."
Key point:
- Receivables affect both cash planning and financial reporting.
- Aging is useful only when every overdue item has a documented action.

5. Q: Why are bank reconciliations a management control rather than a routine clerical exercise?
A: Show answer
The bank is an independent source of cash information, while the accounting ledger represents the organization's internal record. A Finance Manager should use the reconciliation to identify unrecorded receipts, unpresented payments, bank charges, timing differences, duplicate postings, and unexplained movements. GWAHS explicitly assigns bank reconciliations to the finance function, so the control should have a documented monthly timetable, preparer and reviewer separation where practical, and evidence that reconciling items are cleared. A reconciliation that merely proves the closing balances agree is insufficient if old reconciling items remain unresolved. Power Query can standardize bank exports and compare them with the cashbook using reference, amount, date, and transaction type. VBA can prepare an exception sheet showing unmatched transactions and the age of each item. The Finance Manager should review unusual cash movements and ensure that significant unresolved items are escalated before reporting is finalized.

Send to the team:
- "Please complete bank reconciliations to a defined timetable and clear or escalate all aged reconciling items before close."
Key point:
- A bank reconciliation is an independent check on the cash ledger.
- The quality of the investigation into exceptions matters as much as the reconciliation itself.

7. Q: Why must accruals be based on the period to which costs relate rather than the payment date?
A: Show answer
The close process should reflect the financial activity of the reporting period even where an invoice or payment arrives later. A Finance Manager should identify services received before period end, confirm the basis for estimation, document the supporting evidence, and reverse or settle the accrual in a controlled manner in the following period. In grant-funded programs, this is particularly important where donor reports depend on a defined reporting window. An unsupported accrual can create the opposite problem by overstating expenditure or charging a grant before the cost is sufficiently evidenced. The control should therefore include the reason for the accrual, calculation basis, project or grant code, responsible budget holder, supporting evidence, and review date. Power Query can compare unmatched purchase, service, and invoice records to identify likely accrual candidates. VBA can generate the accrual schedule and reversal checklist, while leaving final approval to finance management.

Send to the team:
- "Please document the service period, calculation basis, funding code, and review date for every material accrual."
Key point:
- Accruals improve period accuracy but require evidence and review.
- Grant charging should follow both accounting timing and donor eligibility requirements.

GWAHS also requires annual budgeting and budget monitoring across grants and programs. A sound process records the original budget, approved amendments, current forecast, and remaining funding. Power Query can consolidate approved budget versions and actual results, while VBA can generate variance summaries and change logs. Management should review whether a revised budget reflects genuine operational change or simply masks unfavorable performance.

Send to the team:
