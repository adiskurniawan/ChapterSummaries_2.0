Donor Agreement Review and Grant Setup Controls
1. Q: What is the purpose of a donor agreement review and grant setup SOP?
A: Show answer
The SOP establishes a controlled process for evaluating donor funding instruments before the organization commits to their terms, approving the correct agreement and budget versions, obtaining authorized signatures, configuring grant master data, and transferring enforceable obligations to operational owners. It helps ensure that legal commitments, financial assumptions, donor restrictions, reporting duties, and system configurations remain consistent. The process should prevent premature signing, acceptance of funds, expenditure commitments, and activation when required reviews or approvals remain incomplete. It should also provide traceable evidence of the decision and explain why an opportunity was approved, conditionally approved, held, deferred, or declined. The supplied SOP is explicitly a fictional demonstration: its parties, clauses, approvals, budgets, and evidence records must never be presented as genuine organizational records or authority to proceed.

Key point:
- Agreement review and grant setup require documented authorization and evidence.
- The supplied example is fictional and must not be used as a live approval record.
2. Q: Which transactions and lifecycle events should fall within the SOP's scope?
A: Show answer
Scope should cover prospective funding opportunities, draft agreements, award letters, executed agreements, restricted donations, renewals, consortium arrangements, amendments, extensions, and other funding instruments requiring formal review or grant configuration. The scope register should identify the recipient legal entity, funding arrangement, applicable processes, responsible owner, and relevant approval route. The SOP should also identify exclusions so users do not assume that agreement review automatically authorizes journal posting, grant revenue recognition, procurement, payroll, partner management, tax filings, or donor reporting. Those activities remain governed by their respective processes. Where another procedure becomes responsible, the handoff should include the agreement version, relevant clauses, required evidence, owner, and referral reference.

3. Q: How should governing law, donor agreements, internal policies, and accounting frameworks be distinguished?
A: Show answer
Applicable legislation and regulatory requirements must be identified first because a contract or internal approval cannot override mandatory law. The executed agreement and its incorporated documents then establish the contractual commitments, subject to applicable law and interpretation. Internal policies, delegated authority, and organizational risk limits govern whether the organization may accept and perform those commitments. Accounting recognition and measurement are separate decisions based on the applicable financial reporting framework and the transaction's actual facts. When requirements conflict, the reviewer should document the conflicting provisions, identify the decision required, obtain qualified legal or technical advice, and keep the matter on hold if it prevents an authorized decision. The hierarchy must be verified for the actual entity and instrument rather than assumed from the fictional example.

Key point:
- Contractual acceptance and accounting treatment are distinct decisions.
- Mandatory legal requirements cannot be waived through internal approval.

Incomplete requests should be returned or held with an explicit reason rather than treated as ready for approval.

6. Q: Why is donor and counterparty due diligence necessary before agreement approval?
A: Show answer
Due diligence helps the organization assess whether the proposed relationship is legitimate, within its mandate, and manageable under its legal, operational, financial, and integrity requirements. Depending on the arrangement and risk, checks may include legal identity, authority to enter the agreement, beneficial ownership where relevant, sanctions screening, conflicts of interest, fraud indicators, financial capacity, safeguarding exposure, and reputational concerns. The actual checks must follow applicable law, donor conditions, internal policy, and the risk-based review procedure. Record the source, screening date, scope, result, reviewer, and disposition. A search result is not automatically a clearance, and an inconclusive match is not equivalent to a confirmed negative result. Unresolved material concerns should remain on hold until the authorized specialist documents a decision and its basis.

Key point:
- Screening requires evidence and an authorized disposition.
- Unresolved material risks must not be converted into an assumed clearance.
7. Q: What should happen when donor screening produces a possible sanctions match or incomplete result?
A: Show answer
Preserve the screening result and the information needed for authorized investigation in an appropriately restricted record. The Compliance or Legal specialist should compare relevant identifiers, assess the quality and limitations of the match, obtain necessary authoritative checks, and document the final disposition. Do not describe a possible match as cleared solely because it has not been confirmed. Until required clearance is obtained, block the affected approval, transaction, or activation step according to the organization's risk procedure. Avoid unnecessary disclosure of sensitive investigation details to general grant users. If a legal reporting duty or an urgent protective action may apply, escalate through the designated process without waiting for routine agreement review to finish.

These are review prompts, not a universal list of contractual requirements. Every material clause must be reviewed or marked not applicable with a documented rationale and authorized acceptance. Unresolved conflicts, missing clauses, or unacceptable terms must remain visible in the decision pack.

9. Q: How should responsibilities be allocated across Program, Grants, Finance, Legal, Operations, and Management?

Authorized management accepts risks within its delegation, and authorized signatories execute the approved instrument. 10. Q: How should segregation of duties be applied during agreement approval and grant setup?
A: Show answer
Separate preparation, independent review, approval, signature, system implementation, and post-implementation verification wherever practicable. A requester should not be the sole person approving the request, accepting its own unresolved exceptions, or certifying its own grant setup. A person who changes the approved budget should not independently validate that same change without an authorized compensating control. If staffing constraints prevent full separation, document the conflict, assess the risk, obtain approval for the alternative arrangement, and introduce an independent check appropriate to the risk. Review actual system permissions as well as the written responsibility matrix because technical access can undermine formal segregation. Keep the resulting evidence with the relevant approval and configuration record.

11. Q: What is the difference between a donor restriction and a donor-imposed condition for accounting purposes?
A: Show answer
A restriction limits or directs the purpose for which transferred resources may be used. A condition, under the framework described in the UN IPSAS Finance Manual, involves a performance obligation together with an enforceable return obligation if the specified requirement is not fulfilled. This distinction matters because a restriction does not automatically create the same accounting consequences as a condition. Review the actual clause, enforceability of any return obligation, related performance requirements, and the applicable accounting framework. Record the contractual restriction in the grant controls regardless of whether it changes revenue recognition. Do not classify an arrangement based only on words such as “restricted grant,” “conditional funding,” or “donation”; the specific terms and applicable reporting requirements must be assessed.

- The precise agreement and applicable accounting guidance determine the treatment.

12. Q: Why must legal agreement approval be kept separate from grant revenue recognition?
A: Show answer
Agreement approval establishes that the organization has completed the required decision process and may accept the contractual terms within its authority. Accounting recognition is a separate assessment of the applicable framework, the rights obtained, control of resources, enforceable obligations, conditions, and transaction facts. The UN IPSAS Finance Manual explains that accounting for non-exchange transfers requires assessment of whether an asset or liability arises and whether conditions affect the amount recognized as revenue. The relevant framework for the actual organization must be confirmed rather than automatically applying UN-specific operational policies to another entity. Record the accounting-policy referral, preparer, reviewer, conclusion, and supporting clauses independently from the approval to sign and the technical grant setup.

17. Q: How should the organization distinguish report preparation, submission, receipt, and acceptance?
A: Show answer
Define separate workflow states for each meaningful event. A report may be prepared, under review, approved, submitted, received, acknowledged, accepted, or returned for correction. These states are not interchangeable: evidence that an email was sent does not establish that the donor received the report, and receipt does not necessarily establish acceptance when the agreement requires formal approval. Store the final submitted version, submission timestamp, recipient or portal reference, receipt evidence, and any donor response. When a report is rejected or challenged, retain the original and revised versions, assign corrective actions, and update the obligation status only when the applicable completion criteria are met. The status model must match the agreement's actual requirements.

20. Q: What are conditions precedent, and how should they affect agreement execution and grant activation?
A: Show answer
A condition precedent is a specified requirement that must be satisfied before a contractual or operational step may proceed, according to the actual instrument and applicable law. Examples may include a required donor approval, receipt of a formal document, completion of a specified clearance, or evidence of an agreed prerequisite. Record each condition's source clause, responsible owner, deadline, required evidence, reviewer, and consequences if unmet. Distinguish conditions to signing, conditions to effectiveness, conditions to drawdown, and conditions to expenditure or other activities because they may trigger different restrictions. The status must remain on hold for the affected action until authorized evidence establishes that the condition has been satisfied or that an authorized resolution permits another course.

3. Q: Why must the applicable accounting framework be verified before establishing donor receivable recognition and impairment rules?
A: Show answer
Recognition, measurement, impairment, presentation, and derecognition depend on the financial reporting framework applicable to the reporting entity. The UN IPSAS Finance Manual describes accounting policies and Umoja procedures for the United Nations; it is a useful reference for an NGO, but its specific rules and system procedures do not automatically become mandatory for an independent Indonesian foundation or international NGO. The organization must establish which accounting standards, approved accounting policies, donor conditions, contractual provisions, and mandatory local requirements apply. The policy owner should document the authority hierarchy, the specific requirements adopted, and any unresolved interpretations. 12. Q: What is the difference between a potential claim, a submitted claim, an accepted claim, and a recognized receivable?
A: Show answer
A potential claim is an expected or conditional funding amount that has not yet passed the relevant claim or accounting gates. A submitted claim has been dispatched through an approved channel, but submission alone does not establish donor acceptance. An accepted claim has evidence that the donor accepted the request according to the applicable process; the precise contractual and accounting implications must still be evaluated. A recognized receivable is an asset recorded after the applicable accounting recognition criteria have been met. These statuses may be related but are not interchangeable. The organization should maintain separate operational and accounting status fields and define the evidence required for each transition. A portal status or claim acknowledgment must not automatically post revenue, create a receivable, or clear an existing balance without the relevant authorized accounting decision.

13. Q: How does a donor receivable differ from grant revenue or a forecast of future funding?
A: Show answer
A receivable represents an asset when the applicable accounting recognition criteria for a right to receive resources have been satisfied. Revenue represents a recognized increase in economic resources or service potential, subject to the applicable framework and any associated liabilities. A forecast represents an expected future amount and does not, by itself, create either recognized revenue or a receivable. Under the UN's non-exchange revenue policy, asset recognition and any liability associated with donor conditions affect the amount of revenue recognized; other entities must apply their own governing framework and policies. Staff should document the underlying right, conditions, amount, timing, and accounting basis rather than classify all anticipated donor funding as an asset.

Confirm the legal entity, accounting framework, jurisdiction, signed agreement, approved budget, effective amendments, applicable donor requirements, internal policies, and authorized roles. Validate every workflow, control, form, status transition, formula, source link, and test against the real operating environment. Run realistic positive and negative tests, reconcile outputs independently, and record any limitations or unresolved issues. Obtain review from the responsible finance, grants, legal, tax, asset, or other specialists as relevant. Secure formal adoption by authorized management and record the effective date, owner, review schedule, and approval evidence. Until these steps are complete, clearly label the procedure as a controlled draft, not an approved SOP.

Study instruction:
- "Create an adoption checklist separating verified organizational facts, fictional assumptions, mandatory tests, outstanding approvals, and release evidence."
Key point:
- A detailed fictional procedure is a design reference, not evidence of actual compliance.
- Formal adoption requires verified applicability, testing, documented approvals, and an effective version.
