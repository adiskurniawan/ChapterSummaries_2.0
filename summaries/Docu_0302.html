1. Q: What are the responsibilities of Requesters and Travellers throughout the travel request and advance process?
A: Show answer
The Requester is responsible for creating the Travel Request (TR), completing General Info, adding Travellers, entering expected fee lines, providing supporting documents where required, and submitting the request. The Traveller is responsible for confirming an approved travel advance when applicable, entering the amount needed for each eligible advance line, selecting the currency, and providing the required note. These roles may belong to the same person or to different individuals. When the Requester also travels, the system still distinguishes the request-management responsibilities from the traveller-specific advance confirmation responsibilities. The TRO manages the operational review and completion stage, while the PI(s) approve their assigned fee lines. Finance performs the final advance confirmation and closure process. Each role must act only within its assigned permissions. Users should not bypass approval stages by using another person's account or performing actions outside their assigned authority. The Workflow History provides the record of actions, users, and timestamps throughout the process.

Key point:
- Request creation, fee approval, advance confirmation, and financial closure are separate responsibilities.
- Users must operate within their assigned permissions and approval authority.

2. Q: How should a normal Travel Request move from initial preparation through submission, review, approval, and final confirmation?
A: Show answer
The normal workflow begins when the Requester creates the TR and saves its General Info. The application then generates the TR number and moves the Requester to the Travellers tab. The Requester adds at least one Traveller, enters the expected costs in the Fees tab, uploads required attachments, completes the Travel Justification when enabled, and submits the request. After successful submission, the TR becomes Submitted and the TRO receives a notification with the Requester copied. The TRO reviews the request, updates costs and supporting documents where necessary, and completes the operational review. The TR then becomes Pending Approval and its relevant fee lines are routed to their assigned PI(s). Once all fee lines entering approval have been approved, the TR becomes Approved. Where a travel advance applies, an additional confirmation and financial closure workflow follows. Each stage has its own validation rules, responsible user, status, and evidence. The workflow should not be treated as a single approval action because successful submission does not mean that the request is fully approved or that an advance has been confirmed.

Study instruction:
- "Trace one TR from creation through final approval and identify every status transition, responsible role, and system validation."

Key point:
- Submission, operational completion, PI approval, and advance closure are separate workflow stages.
- A TR is approved only when all fee lines entering approval satisfy the final approval gate.

3. Q: What information must be completed before a Requester can begin adding Travellers and fee lines?

General Info must be completed and saved first. This initial save establishes the TR record, generates its identification number, and allows the Requester to continue to the Travellers and Fees tabs. The system's initial save does not represent financial approval or authorization to travel. It only establishes the draft record and enables the remaining preparation activities.

4. Q: How should Travellers be added to a Travel Request when one person makes several travel legs?
A: Show answer
The Requester must add every individual who will travel, and at least one Traveller is required before submission. The application does not allow the same person to be added twice to one TR. Therefore, when the same individual undertakes several connected travel legs within one request, use the departure date of the first leg as the overall Departure date and the return date of the last leg as the overall Return date. The Return date must not be earlier than the Departure date. This structure avoids duplicate Traveller records and provides an overall travel period for the request. Before submitting, the Requester should verify that all Travellers are included, names and identities are correct, and the overall dates reflect the intended itinerary. Changes in travel arrangements should be handled through the applicable editing or reopening process rather than by creating duplicate Traveller entries to represent each leg.

6. Q: What conditions must be satisfied before a Requester can submit a Travel Request?
A: Show answer
Submission is permitted only when the TR contains at least one Traveller, the Travel Justification is fully answered when the relevant team has enabled it, and every approval code selected in General Info still has available budget. The budget rule is strict: Remaining must be greater than zero for every selected approval code. If even one code has a Remaining balance of zero or less, submission is blocked. The application displays the problems so the Requester can identify the affected fields or codes. The system also performs its budget check when the Requester confirms submission in the dialog. These validations are preventive controls intended to stop incomplete requests and requests associated with unavailable budget. The Requester should correct missing information or resolve the budget issue through the appropriate financial authority. Repeatedly pressing Submit without correcting the underlying issue will not resolve the validation failure.

Key point:
- At least one Traveller and any required Travel Justification are mandatory.
- Every selected approval code must have Remaining greater than zero.

7. Q: What happens after the Requester successfully submits the TR?
A: Show answer
After successful submission, the TR status changes from Draft to Submitted. The Requester can no longer edit the TR and can only view it while the TRO and PI(s) perform their assigned activities. The TRO receives the submission email, with the Requester copied. The submission creates an official workflow record and establishes the point at which operational review begins. The Requester should therefore check the accuracy of the request before confirming submission because changes may subsequently require the TRO to return the request or use an approved reopening procedure. A Submitted status must not be interpreted as proof that the travel has been fully approved, that every fee line has passed approval, or that a travel advance has been confirmed. The next action belongs to the TRO, who checks the request and determines whether it is ready to proceed.

8. Q: What checks must the TRO complete before sending a submitted TR to the PI(s)?
A: Show answer
The TRO must ensure that the TR has at least one fee line and that every approval code selected in General Info is used by at least one fee line. At least one OUCRU fee line must not yet have been sent for approval. Every approval code associated with the lines being submitted must have an assigned approver; missing approval codes are listed by the system. In addition, each fee type selected in Travel Advance must have at least one OUCRU fee line of that type. Other Sources fee lines cannot satisfy this requirement. Finally, the system checks whether each approval code has sufficient budget for the total OUCRU cost being sent for that code. Budget checks run last, after the other validations. The TRO should review the errors and correct the relevant records before attempting completion again. Completing the TR is not simply an administrative forwarding action; it is a controlled validation and routing stage.

9. Q: How should the PI approval process work when several fee lines or several PIs are involved?
A: Show answer
The PI approval process operates at fee-line level rather than treating the entire TR as one indivisible approval item. Each PI receives only the fee lines assigned to that PI. Individual lines can be approved separately, and the application records the outcome of each approval action. The TR becomes Approved only when every fee line that entered approval has reached Approved status. If at least one line remains Pending Approval or Returned, the TR remains Pending Approval, even when other lines have already been approved. Previously approved lines remain saved and do not need to be approved again merely because another line is still outstanding. This design allows multiple approval responsibilities to operate in parallel while preserving a single overall TR status. The Requester should monitor the outstanding line-level approvals rather than assuming that one PI's approval completes the entire request.

10. Q: What additional conditions apply when a PI approves their own travel request or a TR marked as PI travels?
A: Show answer
When the person approving is also a Traveller in the TR, or the TR is marked PI travels, that PI must select a PEC and enter a comment before approving. In this circumstance, the PI's approval becomes the first approval in the process. This is an additional governance condition rather than a routine fee-line approval requirement for every PI. The approving PI must not assume that being eligible to approve their own lines removes the additional requirements. The application should be checked to ensure that the PEC selection and comment are captured correctly. 11. Q: How should the final approval gate determine whether the overall TR becomes Approved?
A: Show answer
The final approval gate evaluates whether every fee line that entered approval has been approved. It does not prevent an individual PI from saving a valid approval merely because another line remains outstanding. If a PI approves their assigned lines while another line is Pending Approval or Returned, the approved lines remain saved and the overall TR continues to display Pending Approval. The Approved email is not sent at that stage. Once every line entering approval is Approved, the overall TR changes to Approved and the completion notification is triggered. This distinction prevents the system from reporting an overall approval while some financial components remain unresolved. Reviewers should therefore distinguish between an approved line and an approved TR when examining the application, monitoring outstanding approvals, and communicating travel authorization status.

17. Q: What is the difference between deleting a Draft TR and cancelling a previously submitted TR?
A: Show answer
Deletion applies to a Draft TR that has never been submitted. The Delete action removes the TR and its associated fee lines, Travellers, and files permanently. Cancellation applies to a TR that has already passed through the workflow at least once and has subsequently returned to Draft. The Requester can use Cancel TR in that situation and must provide a mandatory reason. The record becomes Cancelled and its history remains available. The distinction is important because deletion removes an unsubmitted preparation record, while cancellation preserves the history of a request that has entered the controlled workflow. A user should not choose deletion simply because a submitted request is no longer needed. The previous submission history and the current status determine which action is available.

23. Q: How is the maximum advance amount available to an individual Traveller calculated?
A: Show answer
The amount that a Traveller can request is limited by Remaining for you. This represents the balance available on the line after considering amounts already taken by other Travellers. The names of those other Travellers are hidden, but their confirmed amounts affect the remaining availability. The Traveller's requested amount is converted into the approval code's currency before the comparison is applied, and the converted amount is rounded to two decimal places. The requested amount must not exceed the available balance. The Traveller should not calculate the limit solely from the original approved fee amount because that amount may already have been partly allocated to other people. The application performs the relevant validation using the available balance and the exchange rate. If the amount is higher than the permitted balance, confirmation is blocked and the amount must be corrected.

A PI is responsible for reviewing and approving the fee lines assigned to them through their approval codes. When a PI receives a Travel Request (TR) for approval, they must check the relevant fee lines, verify the amounts, approval codes, descriptions, budget availability, and applicable exchange rates, and then approve or reject their assigned lines. A PEC performs a similar approval function for fee lines delegated to them by a PI. The PEC acts as the second approver when the PI is also a traveller on the TR or when the Requester indicates that a PI is one of the travellers. The key distinction is that a PI approves the lines originally assigned to them, whereas a PEC approves the lines handed over by the PI. The PEC cannot delegate those lines further. The Travel Request Officer (TRO) manages submission, correction, resubmission, and reassignment activities, while Finance (FIN) provides the budget information used in the approval checks.

2. Q: How does the normal approval process work when a Travel Request contains fee lines for only one PI?
A: Show answer
When every fee line belongs to one PI, the process follows a straightforward sequence. First, the Requester submits the TR. The TRO completes the request and sends the applicable OUCRU fee lines to the PI assigned to each approval code. The PI receives an email notification and can see the request in Approvals. The TR changes to Pending Approval, and the relevant fee lines also show Pending Approval. The PI opens the TR, reviews the assigned lines, and approves them. Once all lines requiring approval have been approved, and no other PI or PEC approval remains outstanding, the TR becomes Approved. The approved fee lines show Approved as their status. The system sends the final approval notification to the Requester, with the TRO copied. This is the standard single-PI workflow, provided that all submission and approval validations pass.

3. Q: What is the difference between approving an entire Travel Request and approving individual fee lines?
A: Show answer
The approval decision is made at fee-line level, while the TR status reflects the combined approval position of all fee lines that entered the approval workflow. A PI does not automatically approve every fee line in a TR simply because they approve one line or because they are the primary investigator. They approve only the lines assigned to them. When several PIs are involved, each PI approves their own assigned lines independently. The TR remains Pending Approval while any fee line is still Pending Approval or Returned. The TR becomes Approved only when every line that entered approval has reached Approved status. This distinction is essential because an individual PI may have completed their responsibility even though the overall TR is not yet approved. The system therefore preserves approved fee lines while waiting for the remaining approvals.

The Complete action sends eligible OUCRU fee lines into the approval workflow. Therefore, at least one OUCRU fee line must not have been sent for approval previously. 30. Q: What happens when a PI's approval is blocked because the budget is insufficient?
A: Show answer
When the PI presses Approve, the system displays a budget-checking message such as Processing budget checking. If the check fails, the system lists the affected approval codes and explains the budget problem.
