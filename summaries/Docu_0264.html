A: Show answer

The objective is to move from "we have a procedure" to "we have evidence that the procedure works."

11. Q: How would you review transactions for completeness, accuracy, reasonability, and allowability before approval?
A: Show answer
WCS expressly requires the Finance Manager to review accounting transactions against four dimensions: completeness, accuracy, reasonability, and allowability. I would use those as separate review questions. Completeness asks whether all required documents and approvals are present. Accuracy tests amount, coding, calculation, tax, and accounting treatment. Reasonability considers whether the transaction makes sense in context, such as price, timing, purpose, and frequency. Power Query can flag missing fields, invalid project codes, duplicates, unusual amounts, or transactions outside expected dates; VBA can route exceptions for review rather than automatically approving them. The purpose is not to turn finance into an administrative bottleneck but to ensure that automated processing does not replace professional judgment.

Power Query can reduce repeated data preparation, and VBA can maintain standardized checklists and status reports, but the process should remain understandable to another trained staff member. This is particularly important in project environments where start-up, implementation, and close-out can create rapidly changing priorities.
