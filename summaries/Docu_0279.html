A: Show answer
The SOP establishes a management-owned process for preparing financial records, reconciliations, supporting schedules, financial statements, disclosures, audit evidence, external confirmations, and responses to auditor information requests. 2. Q: How should an NGO distinguish internal audit readiness from completion of the external audit?
A: Show answer
Internal audit readiness means that management has completed the internal preparation activities defined by its approved checklist. Examples include reconciling material balances, assembling supporting schedules, resolving outstanding close items, and preparing financial statement drafts. It does not establish that the auditor has obtained sufficient appropriate evidence, completed testing, accepted proposed adjustments, or issued an opinion. External audit completion must be evidenced through the actual engagement deliverables and applicable reporting documentation. Maintain separate statuses for internal preparation, auditor review, outstanding requests, management approval, auditor report issuance, required filing, and final handover. Do not allow a completed internal checklist to automatically update the external audit status to complete. Each transition must have its own evidence and authorization criteria.

Study instruction:
- "Compare an internally completed audit-readiness checklist with the evidence needed to demonstrate actual audit completion and report issuance."
Key point:
- Internal readiness and independent audit completion are different control states.
- Every final status must be supported by evidence appropriate to that status.

4. Q: How should management determine whether a statutory audit is legally required for an Indonesian foundation?

5. Q: How should statutory audits, donor audits, group audits, voluntary audits, reviews, and agreed-upon procedures be distinguished?
A: Show answer
Identify the engagement type before defining its calendar, deliverables, evidence requirements, and completion criteria. A statutory financial statement audit addresses applicable legal and financial reporting requirements. A donor or contractual audit is governed by relevant award terms and the agreed engagement scope, which may not be identical to a statutory audit. Group or component audits introduce additional reporting-perimeter and auditor-coordination requirements. Voluntary audits arise from an organizational decision or another applicable commitment, while reviews and agreed-upon procedures have different objectives and reporting outputs from an audit opinion. Document the engagement's subject matter, criteria, intended users, report addressee, period, required procedures, and authority. Do not combine several engagements into one generic status or assume that satisfying one engagement automatically satisfies another.

Key point:
- Engagement type determines which requirements and deliverables must be satisfied.
- An audit, review, and agreed-upon procedures engagement are not interchangeable.

7. Q: How should management responsibilities be separated from the independent auditor's responsibilities?
A: Show answer
Management is responsible for maintaining accounting records, preparing financial statements under the applicable framework, establishing internal controls, making accounting judgments, assessing going concern and subsequent events, supplying complete and truthful information, and approving management decisions. The independent auditor is responsible for independently planning and performing audit procedures, assessing risks, evaluating evidence, making audit judgments, and expressing a conclusion in the appropriate report. Management must not present an auditor's preliminary comment as a final opinion or require the auditor to assume management's accounting responsibilities. Conversely, management cannot delegate responsibility for its own financial statements merely by appointing an external audit firm. The SOP should state these boundaries clearly and define how auditor observations, requests, proposed adjustments, and findings are communicated and resolved without compromising independence.

Study instruction:
- "Assign management and auditor responsibilities to every major audit activity and identify any task that would compromise independent review."
Key point:
- Management owns the records, statements, controls, and representations it provides.
- The external auditor retains responsibility for independent procedures and the audit conclusion.

14. Q: Why must trial-balance versions be preserved instead of overwritten after submission?
A: Show answer
Each issued trial balance represents a specific set of account balances for a defined entity, period, and accounting state. Assign a unique version identifier and record extraction date, source ledger, period, currency, preparer, reviewer, control totals, and approval status. When a late journal or correction changes balances, retain the previously issued version and create a new version linked to the authorized change. Record the reason, affected accounts, posting reference, reviewer, and effects on schedules or financial statements. Overwriting a previously submitted file can make it impossible to reconstruct which numbers supported earlier audit procedures or management approvals. Version control therefore supports traceability and ensures that reconciliations, statements, and audit requests tie to the precise ledger state they were designed to support.

15. Q: How should the trial balance be reconciled to the general ledger and subsidiary records?
A: Show answer
Confirm that the trial balance was generated from the correct ledger, legal entity, reporting period, account set, and currency. Reconcile debit and credit totals, validate account balances against the general ledger detail, and agree relevant control accounts to subsidiary records such as receivables, payables, and fixed assets. Verify interface postings and identify transactions that are missing, duplicated, parked, posted to the wrong period, or recorded against inappropriate accounts. A balanced trial balance proves only arithmetic equality between debits and credits; it does not establish that the underlying population is complete or correctly classified. Document the reconciliation method, source files, control totals, differences, corrections, and independent review. Unexplained material differences should prevent the affected audit-readiness gate from being cleared.
