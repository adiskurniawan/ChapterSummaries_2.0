Payroll Management, Controls, and Reconciliation
1. Q: What is the purpose of a comprehensive payroll management SOP?
A: Show answer
A payroll management Standard Operating Procedure establishes a controlled, documented process from authorized personnel changes through payroll calculation, independent review, salary payment, accounting, reconciliation, exception resolution, and record retention. Its purpose is to ensure that employees are paid accurately and on time, deductions and employer contributions are determined correctly, payment instructions are authorized, accounting entries are complete, and sensitive information is protected. The SOP should identify responsible roles, required evidence, approval points, system states, exception handling, and closure criteria. It must also distinguish an approved procedure from a draft or fictional example. The supplied SOP is explicitly a fictional demonstration and has not been adopted. It cannot be treated as evidence of actual organizational approval, legal compliance, payroll payment, or accounting execution.
Study instruction:
- Trace the complete payroll cycle and identify the control, responsible person, and evidence required at every stage.
Key point:
- A payroll SOP governs the complete process, not merely the salary calculation.
- Operational adoption requires verified policies, applicable law, and authorized approval.

3. Q: What activities should fall within the controlled payroll process, and which require separate procedures?
A: Show answer

5. Q: Why must the authoritative source and payroll-parameter register be maintained separately from payroll calculations?
A: Show answer
A parameter register identifies the legal or policy source for each calculation rule, including its effective date, applicability, owner, reviewer, approval status, version, and permitted use. Parameters may include wage components, tax treatment, contribution bases, applicable rates, caps, benefit eligibility, rounding conventions, and payroll calendars. The payroll calculation should consume only parameters that have passed the required source and applicability reviews. An unverified value must remain on hold rather than becoming active simply because it exists in a spreadsheet or system. When a rule changes, preserve the old version, identify affected payroll periods, test the new version, approve the change, and activate it only on the authorized effective date. This makes calculations reproducible and helps reviewers explain differences between periods.

- Select a calculation parameter and trace it from its authoritative source through applicability review, approval, effective date, and actual use.
Key point:
- Every active parameter needs an identifiable source and approved applicability.
- Unverified or superseded values must not silently influence payroll calculations.

6. Q: How should payroll responsibilities be divided between HR, payroll, finance, Treasury, IT, and management?
A: Show answer
HR or the authorized employment decision owner verifies employment status and approves personnel changes within delegated authority. Payroll prepares inputs and calculations. An independent reviewer checks completeness, calculations, exceptions, and supporting evidence. An authorized approver approves the specified payroll version. Treasury prepares the payment batch, while authorized bank signatories release payments under the approved bank mandate. Accounting prepares or reviews payroll journals and checks the actual posting. The designated controller performs reconciliation and certification. Tax and benefits specialists determine applicable statutory treatments, while IT administers systems only through approved access controls. Management assigns authority and monitors significant exceptions. Internal Audit or another independent assurance function tests controls without operating the controls being reviewed. Each role must have documented permissions and explicit limitations.
Study instruction:
- Prepare a responsibility matrix identifying who requests, prepares, reviews, approves, executes, posts, reconciles, and independently tests each payroll activity.
Key point:
- Accountability must follow documented responsibilities and delegated authority.
- System access or seniority alone does not establish authority to approve payroll.
7. Q: Why is segregation of duties important in payroll management?
A: Show answer
Payroll involves confidential records, employee compensation, bank instructions, statutory deductions, and accounting entries. If one person can create an employee, change compensation, calculate pay, approve the register, release funds, and reconcile the results, that person may be able to commit or conceal unauthorized transactions. Separate incompatible duties wherever practicable. In particular, the requester should not solely approve their own pay change, a payroll preparer should not solely review their own calculation, and a payment preparer should not be the sole signatory for the same payment batch. If staffing limitations prevent full segregation, a formally approved compensating control should include independent review of the affected activity, clear evidence, and periodic testing.
Study instruction:
- Review the payroll access matrix and identify any individual who can both initiate and approve the same material transaction.
Key point:
- Segregation reduces the opportunity for errors, unauthorized changes, and concealment.
- Compensating controls must be documented and independently tested.

22. Q: How should employer costs be distinguished from employee deductions and net salary payable?
A: Show answer
Employee deductions reduce the amount payable to an employee when properly authorized and legally permissible. Employer contributions and other employer-only costs are separate expenses or cost components under the applicable accounting policy and statutory requirements. Employee withholding and contribution amounts may create liabilities that the employer must subsequently remit. The net salary payable represents the amount owed to the employee after relevant authorized deductions and adjustments. These amounts must be separately identified in the calculation, payment, journal, and reconciliation records. The accounting design should prevent a deduction from being counted twice as both a reduction in salary payable and an additional employer cost. Unlawful, disputed, or unsupported deductions must be held for appropriate review rather than automatically processed.
