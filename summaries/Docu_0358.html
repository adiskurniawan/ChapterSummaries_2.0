9. Q: What are diminishing returns in skill development?
A: Show answer
Diminishing returns occur when each additional unit of effort produces a smaller improvement than the previous unit, assuming other conditions remain similar. Early practice may produce rapid gains in basic technique, while later practice yields smaller refinements requiring disproportionate time. The chapter uses guitar practice to illustrate how the pursuit of increasingly high performance can become inefficient. Diminishing returns do not mean improvement stops; they mean the rate of improvement declines. A learner should recognize when a skill has reached a useful level for the current goal and consider whether another capability would create greater total benefit.

Study instruction:
- Track practice time and observable improvement over several weeks to identify whether gains are accelerating, stable, or diminishing.

Key point:
- A declining rate of improvement is not the same as zero improvement.
- Continuing should be judged against the value of other possible investments.
