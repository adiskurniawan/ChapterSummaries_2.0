16. Q: What is a genetic algorithm?
A: Show answer
A genetic algorithm is a heuristic optimization method inspired by natural selection. Candidate solutions are treated like a population, and selection favors fitter candidates for reproduction. Variation mechanisms such as mutation and recombination create new candidates, and the process can repeat over generations. The method belongs to evolutionary computation and is useful for optimization and combinatorial problems where directly searching the entire solution space may be impractical.

72. Q: How should the exploration rate be monitored during RL training?

73. Q: How can the Bellman equation support troubleshooting when an agent learns a poor policy?

The Bellman relationship provides a structured way to inspect whether value estimates are consistent with observed rewards and next-state values. 74. Q: What is the difference between a Markov chain and an MDP in a practical control problem?
A: Show answer
A Markov chain describes probabilistic movement among states when the transition process is not selected by a decision-making agent. An MDP adds actions that influence which transition probabilities apply and rewards that express the desirability of outcomes. For example, a Markov chain might model traffic states under a fixed signal schedule. An MDP can model a controller that chooses signal timing and receives rewards related to traffic flow or waiting time. The distinction matters because a chain describes dynamics, whereas an MDP provides a decision framework. If actions do not actually affect transitions or rewards, introducing an MDP may add unnecessary complexity.
Study instruction:
- "Model the same small traffic system first as a Markov chain with fixed behavior and then as an MDP with controllable signal actions."
Key point:
- A Markov chain models probabilistic state evolution; an MDP models controlled state evolution with rewards.
- Use an MDP when decisions can influence future outcomes.

76. Q: How can model-based RL use a learned model to plan ahead?
A: Show answer
Model-based RL builds or uses a representation of how the environment changes after actions and what rewards are likely to result. The agent can then evaluate possible action sequences by looking ahead through the model. This differs from purely model-free approaches that learn values or policies directly from experience without explicitly planning through a transition model. 77. Q: Why are reward, policy, and environment definitions part of the same RL design problem?
A: Show answer
An RL system cannot be evaluated by its algorithm alone. The environment defines what can happen, the action space defines what the agent can control, the reward defines what outcomes are valued, and the policy determines how the agent acts. Changing any one of these can change the task itself. A reward that omits a safety constraint can produce unsafe behavior even when the algorithm is implemented correctly. An action space that excludes a necessary control can make the desired outcome impossible. A state that omits relevant information can make the best policy unlearnable. These elements should be reviewed together before training and whenever observed behavior differs from the intended objective.
