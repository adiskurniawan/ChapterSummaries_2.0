3. Q: Why must an NGO distinguish source verification from source applicability?
A: Show answer
A source may be genuine and authoritative without being applicable to a particular organization, transaction, or reporting period. The specification separates source existence and verification from applicability to the actual entity. For example, an official law concerning Indonesian foundations is relevant if the organization falls within its scope, but its existence does not independently establish the organization's legal form, accounting framework, tax treatment, or audit obligations. Similarly, the UN IPSAS Finance Manual describes accounting policies and processes used by the United Nations. Its discussion of general ledger controls and chart-of-accounts architecture can inform system design, but its existence does not itself make IPSAS mandatory for an Indonesian NGO. A source register should record the issuing authority, exact URL, document title, relevant section, checked date, effective period, jurisdiction, applicability assessment, reviewer, and next review trigger. Where later amendments or more current standards may apply, the original source record should direct the reviewer to the necessary updated material instead of being treated as conclusive evidence.

7. Q: What is the chart of accounts, and how does it support reliable financial reporting?
A: Show answer
A chart of accounts is the controlled catalogue of general ledger accounts used to classify accounting transactions and aggregate balances for financial reporting. It defines account identities, descriptions, classifications, normal balances, posting restrictions, hierarchies, ownership, effective dates, and links to reporting destinations. In the UN IPSAS Finance Manual, the chart of accounts is described in the context of producing IPSAS-compliant financial statements. For an NGO, the exact design must follow its own applicable accounting framework and reporting requirements. A well-designed COA helps ensure that transactions with similar accounting characteristics are recorded consistently, while financial dimensions identify additional facts such as the responsible office, funding pool, donor, grant, or project. The account catalogue should therefore remain separate from funding and operational classifications. New accounts must not be activated simply because a user requests a code; they require a valid accounting purpose, approved ownership, complete mappings, compatible dimensions, and tested system behavior.

10. Q: What is the difference between a natural account and a financial dimension?
A: Show answer
A natural account describes the accounting classification of a transaction, such as operating cash, receivables, payables, equipment, grant income, program costs, or administration costs. A financial dimension describes another attribute of the transaction, such as the legal entity, office, fund, project, donor, grant, or cost centre. These dimensions answer questions that a natural-account code cannot answer on its own. For example, an NGO may record a valid program expense in one natural account but need the project, donor, fund, grant, and office dimensions to determine which report should include it and who is responsible for the cost. Combining every possible attribute into one oversized account code can create unnecessary complexity and complicate changes in donor or organizational structure. The supplied specification consequently keeps natural-account classification and dimensions separate. The accounting system should validate their permitted combinations and maintain their histories independently.

19. Q: Why must every used account map to the required reporting view exactly once unless an approved split rule applies?
A: Show answer
Missing, overlapping, or multiple mappings can cause financial statements to omit balances, count them twice, or place them in the wrong category. Where a split or allocation is authorized, the calculation method, evidence, rounding policy, and reconciliation to the source total must be documented and tested independently.

20. Q: How should financial reporting maps be reconciled and their control totals validated?

11. Q: Why is amount-only matching unsafe for suspense and clearing accounts?
A: Show answer
