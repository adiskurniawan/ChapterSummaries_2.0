2. Q: What is the difference between an audit finding, its underlying criteria, its implications, and the recommended corrective action?
A: An audit finding describes the condition identified during the examination, such as missing signatures, an unsupported transaction, or a procurement transaction without sufficient quotation documentation. Criteria represent the requirements against which that condition is assessed, including approved financial procedures, procurement policies, authorization requirements, applicable legislation, or recognized control practices. Implications describe the potential consequences, such as unsupported expenditure, inaccurate financial reporting, excessive purchasing costs, weak accountability, or exposure to regulatory penalties. Recommendations explain the action management should take to address the underlying weakness. For example, a missing purchase quotation is the condition, the organization's procurement policy is the relevant criterion, reduced price transparency is a potential implication, and introducing a documented quotation process is a corrective action. These elements should remain logically connected. A recommendation should address the actual cause of the finding rather than simply repeat the auditor's observation. Management should also distinguish deficiencies in actual transaction execution from deficiencies in filing, document retrieval, or presentation.
Study instruction:
- "Select one management letter finding and independently identify its condition, governing criteria, risk, root cause, and recommended corrective action."
Key point:
- A finding describes what happened, while criteria explain what should have happened.
- Corrective actions should address the identified cause and control weakness.

3. Q: How should management determine whether an audit finding represents an actual control failure, a documentation problem, or an administrative processing error?
A: Management should investigate the underlying transaction rather than relying exclusively on the wording of an audit observation. An actual control failure occurs when a required authorization, procurement procedure, reconciliation, or other control was not performed. A documentation problem occurs when the control may have been performed but evidence cannot be located, retrieved, or linked to the relevant transaction. An administrative processing error occurs when the underlying transaction is supported but the accounting or document-management process contains an incorrect attachment, reference, date, or classification. These situations create different risks and require different corrective actions. YJKN's general ledger discrepancy illustrates the distinction: the draft explains that an incorrect supporting document had been attached to a transaction even though the expenditure was supported by appropriate documentation. The correct evidence was subsequently submitted to the auditors. Management should preserve the original audit trail, investigate why the incorrect document was attached, verify the actual transaction, and strengthen document matching and review procedures. A corrected attachment does not automatically resolve a wider weakness in document-control processes.
Study instruction:
- "Classify each finding as a control failure, documentation deficiency, or administrative error and identify what additional evidence is needed to substantiate the classification."
Key point:
- Different types of deficiencies require different corrective actions.
- Evidence that a transaction was valid does not automatically demonstrate that the control process was effective.

5. Q: Why should every payment voucher be linked to a complete and independently reviewable transaction file?
A: A payment voucher is the accounting and authorization reference connecting an expenditure to its underlying documents. It should allow a reviewer to identify the supplier or recipient, purpose, project, funding source, approved amount, relevant invoice, supporting evidence, and payment authorization without reconstructing the transaction from unrelated files. When evidence is stored separately without a clear reference, reviewers may mistakenly conclude that documents are missing even when they exist elsewhere. A reliable process should use a unique voucher reference and a consistent supporting-document index. Before releasing payment, the reviewer should verify the completeness of the package and ensure that the evidence supports the amount being paid. Original documents and approved electronic records should remain retrievable after posting. For significant or unusual payments, an independent reviewer should trace the voucher back to the underlying contract, procurement decision, delivery evidence, and authorization.

9. Q: How should Indonesian stamp duty requirements be applied to supplier invoices, receipts, and other financial documents?
A: Stamp duty should be assessed according to the legal category of the document, not solely because a payment or invoice exceeds a particular amount. Indonesian Law No. 10 of 2020 establishes a stamp-duty rate of Rp10,000 per qualifying document. The law includes certain civil documents and documents stating monetary amounts exceeding Rp5,000,000 that acknowledge receipt of money or acknowledge that a debt has been settled or accounted for. PMK No. 78 of 2024 provides implementing provisions concerning stamp-duty objects, the time liability arises, payment, and the responsible party. Therefore, an invoice exceeding Rp5,000,000 should not automatically be assumed to require stamp duty merely because its value exceeds that threshold. Finance must establish whether the document meets the statutory definition and whether an exemption or other specific treatment applies. The procurement and payment procedure should define how qualifying documents are identified and how the appropriate stamp-duty evidence is retained.

Key point:
- The Rp5,000,000 threshold relates to specified monetary documents, not every document with an amount above that value.
- The applicable rate is Rp10,000 per qualifying document under the cited legislation.

18. Q: What is the purpose of a three-way match between a purchase order, supplier invoice, and delivery evidence?
A: A three-way match is a control that compares the authorized purchase commitment, the amount claimed by the supplier, and evidence that the organization received the goods or services. Where the transaction does not normally use a purchase order, an approved alternative control should be defined. Three-way matching reduces duplicate payments, overbilling, payment for undelivered goods, and unauthorized expenditure.

23. Q: What is the difference between an incorrect supporting-document attachment and an incorrect accounting entry?
A: An incorrect attachment occurs when the ledger entry may be accurate but the wrong invoice, receipt, or supporting document has been associated with it. An incorrect accounting entry occurs when the amount, account, period, project, or other posting information is itself wrong. These problems can appear similar during an audit because the ledger and attachment do not agree. However, the corrective action is different. An attachment error requires verification of the correct evidence, controlled replacement or supplementary filing, and stronger document-matching controls. A posting error requires accounting analysis and an authorized correction or reclassification. Both conditions should be recorded in the exception register. Management should avoid assuming that an attachment error is harmless until it has verified that the ledger amount is supported by the actual underlying transaction.
