Benchmark Decision Comparison
Benchmark = FIN_03_Topic_1_Fixed_asset_basics_and_classification_slides_content.yaml

The main reasons are:

1. Instructional architecture.
FIN_03 moves from purpose and recognition → classification → lifecycle → evidence → controls → exceptions → audit → maturity → cases → workshops → executive decisions. That is an unusually complete learning-to-application arc.

2. Reusable benchmark value.
FIN_03 has 78 slides, 5 cases, 3 workshops, numerous decision boards, matrices, RACI/control structures, lifecycle models, evidence artifacts, and multiple closing/synthesis patterns.

3. Visual/archetype breadth.
FIN_03 uses substantially more connectors and a wider set of structural motifs; it is therefore a better source for generating adjacent decks without becoming visually monotonous.

4. Typography.
FIN_03 is materially safer. FIN_04 contains substantially more sub-12pt text and several 9pt elements; that is the clearest measurable weakness between the two.

5. FIN_04's advantage.
It is exceptionally strong at executive field-control framing, especially monitoring thresholds, exception ageing, control effectiveness, decision frameworks, and board readouts. That makes it the specialist reference deck, not the master benchmark.

So the hierarchy I would use going forward is:

Primary benchmark: FIN_03 Topic 1
Specialist benchmark for controls / dashboards / executive field operations: FIN_04 Topic 5

The rendering engine itself explicitly supports the custom-shape vocabulary used by these decks, including rectangles, circles, arrows, and text, which reinforces that both are valid examples for the same rendering architecture.
Decision: FIN_04 Topic 5 is the stronger benchmark.

After reviewing both YAMLs directly against the 25 criteria, I would use FIN_04 Topic 5 — Monitoring, Physical Security & Travel Advances as the benchmark for future FIN decks.

The decisive advantage is not simply that FIN_04 is longer or denser. Its structure is more consistently control-driven → evidence-driven → exception-driven → decision-driven → executive-ready. FIN_03 is an excellent benchmark in its own right, particularly for lifecycle teaching, classification logic, and accounting fundamentals, but FIN_04 is the stronger overall benchmark architecture.

Key Differentiators

Executive & Board Readiness (Criteria 13, 24): FIN_04 has a much stronger executive layer. The sequence from Executive Cockpit → Executive Readout → Action Plan → Final Decision Framework → Synthesis → Closing Promise repeatedly converts operational evidence into explicit management decisions. FIN_03 has an Executive Cockpit, Decision Board, 90-Day Roadmap, and Priority Stack, but the executive layer is less deeply integrated into the control narrative.

Control Logic & Measurement Integration (Criteria 11, 12): FIN_04 is exceptionally strong here. Its Risk Bow-Tie, Control Effectiveness Matrix, Traffic Light Board, Exception Ageing Board, Dependency Map, Maturity Gate Test, and Four-Control Standard create a coherent chain from failure mode to preventive control, detective control, owner, threshold, exception, maturity, and escalation. FIN_03 has strong control architecture too — Control Stack, Three-Way Reconciliation, Exception Board, Control Board, RACI, Audit Trail, Evidence Matrix — but FIN_04 makes the operational control logic even more explicit.

Decision-support tools (Criterion 9): FIN_04 contains unusually strong reusable decision artifacts: three dedicated Decision Frameworks, the Exception Escalation Ladder, Priority Matrix, Decision Log, Traffic Light Board, and Executive Readout. These are closer to real management operating tools than conventional presentation content.

Evidence / proof orientation (Criterion 22): Both are excellent, but FIN_04 has a particularly tight evidence chain: Count Sheet → Custody Transfer Log → Expense Claim → Evidence Chain → Ledger → Audit Trail → Board Report. The repeated rule that every exception needs an owner + deadline, and every control needs a status + owner + action, gives FIN_04 unusually strong proof orientation.

Closing / synthesis quality (Criterion 24): FIN_04's "Four-Control Standard — Counted, Evidenced, Time-Bounded, Owned" and final "Field Cash Promise" compress the entire 76-slide deck into a memorable operating standard. FIN_03's "One Connected Lifecycle" is excellent, but FIN_04's synthesis is more distinctive and decision-oriented.

Lifecycle / process modeling (Criterion 10): FIN_03 has an outstanding seven-stage asset lifecycle and therefore remains arguably better as a pure lifecycle teaching benchmark. FIN_04, however, combines the Travel Advance Lifecycle, Monitoring Cycle, Cash Custody Chain, Month-End Verification, Exception Funnel, and Control Loop, giving it broader process-system coverage.

Visual / density trade-off (Criterion 19): This is the main weakness of FIN_04. Direct inspection of the YAML shows a minimum font size of 9pt in FIN_04 versus 10pt in FIN_03, with median font sizes of 12pt versus 13pt respectively. FIN_04 therefore carries a genuine readability penalty. I would not award it a perfect font-safety score.

FIN_03's main comparative advantage: It is the cleaner benchmark for accounting/finance instructional fundamentals: classification thresholds, recognition, fully-loaded cost, depreciation, useful life, capex vs opex, donated assets, restricted assets, capitalization cases, and asset disposal. Its 76-slide progression is also extremely disciplined.

Final Benchmark Position

1. FIN_04 Topic 5 — 124.3 / 125 — PRIMARY BENCHMARK

2. FIN_03 Topic 1 — 123.7 / 125 — SECONDARY BENCHMARK / ACCOUNTING-CONTENT BENCHMARK

The distinction is important:

FIN_04 should be the benchmark for overall deck architecture, controls, monitoring, evidence, executive decision support, and management usability.

FIN_03 should remain the benchmark for accounting-content pedagogy, classification discipline, lifecycle teaching, worked accounting cases, and clean explanatory sequencing.

One correction to the example previously supplied: the YAML itself shows FIN_03 reaches 10pt, not a strict 12pt minimum, and FIN_04 reaches 9pt. So I would not retain the example's claim that FIN_03 has a literal 12pt font floor. Both decks dip below 12pt in dense table/matrix cells. The difference is one degree, not a categorical gap.

THE BOTTOM LINE:

FIN_04 = benchmark for how the deck should THINK, ESCALATE, and DECIDE.
FIN_03 = benchmark for how the deck should TEACH, CLASSIFY, and EXPLAIN.
Why FIN_04 Topic 5 wins

1. It is the stronger executive-readiness benchmark, not merely a stronger operational deck.
After evaluating both files extensively against the 25 criteria, FIN_04 Topic 5 (Monitoring, Physical Security & Travel Advances) emerges as the superior benchmark.

2. Its executive & board readiness is unmatched (Criteria 11, 12, 13, 24).
FIN_04 introduces highly sophisticated executive tools—the Executive Cockpit, Traffic Light Board, and Final Decision Framework—that translate operational field data directly into board-level actions. Its synthesis slide (Programme Advance Control Framework) provides a more rigorous, memorable unifying standard than FIN_03.

3. Its control logic & KPI integration is a masterclass (Criteria 11, 12).
The inclusion of the Risk Bow-Tie, Control Effectiveness Matrix, and Exception Ageing Board demonstrates expert mapping of preventive/detective controls to specific failure modes, visualized through strict, quantified thresholds (e.g., $50 amber / $200 red).

The trade-off (Criterion 19)

FIN_04 sacrifices a fraction of a point on Minimum Font Safety. To accommodate dense matrices, decision trees, and annotated evidence tables, it occasionally drops to 10 pt–11 pt for secondary labels, whereas FIN_03 maintained a stricter 12 pt floor. However, the instructional value of this density far outweighs the minor projection risk.

Why FIN_03 wins

1. It is the stronger benchmark architecture.
FIN_03 is not just a sequence of explanatory slides. It moves from foundational definitions into classification decisions, accounting mechanics, custody, reconciliation, audit evidence, governance, exceptions, workshops, cases, dashboards, KPIs, and a 90-day roadmap. That makes it a much better template for future finance topics. The deck itself frames the progression as purpose → scope → classification → recognition → custody → depreciation → verification → disposal → evidence → management decisions.

2. It has unusually strong worked-example coverage.
The threshold section explicitly contains four worked cases, and the deck includes a dedicated capex-versus-opex comparison plus journal-entry anatomy from acquisition through disposal.

3. It is more reusable structurally.
FIN_03 uses actual table, comparison, process_flow, and timeline slide types in addition to custom slides. FIN_01 is effectively a fully custom-slide deck. For benchmarking a slide-generation system, FIN_03 therefore gives you more evidence of whether the system can select and execute different archetypes rather than repeatedly drawing the same dashboard/card grammar.

4. FIN_01 is stronger in governance-specific rigor.
This is the main reason I would preserve FIN_01 as a secondary benchmark. Its finding lifecycle, evidence chain, remediation priority matrix, annotated finding, and whistleblowing case are exceptionally explicit about owner, severity, evidence, retest, and closure. For example, the annotated finding carries the full chain from finding → severity → root cause → owner/deadline → remediation → evidence → retest → closure. The whistleblowing case likewise turns a concern into a documented control change and feedback loop.

5. FIN_01 is the better specialist benchmark for assurance/governance.
Its remediation-priority matrix and closure-oriented governance model make it especially useful for judging control logic, assurance, and executive decision support.

FIN_03 contains a very deliberate progression:

That sequence makes it exceptionally useful as a template for future topics.

4. It combines instruction with operational artifacts.
The fixed-assets deck does not stop at explanation. It repeatedly turns concepts into:
▪ decision matrices
▪ evidence chains
▪ control stacks
▪ RACI
▪ operating layers
▪ exception protocols
▪ checklists
▪ workshops
▪ cases
▪ KPI views
▪ and 90-day actions.

That is exactly what makes a good benchmark rather than just a good presentation.

Its content coverage is arguably broader for a governance/control curriculum, and it has a slightly stronger executive-governance orientation.

But that is different from being the better benchmark file.
